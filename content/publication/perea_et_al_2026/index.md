abstract: >
  Conservation bioacoustics—the science of recording and analysing animal
  sounds for biodiversity conservation—has become an important component
  of modern conservation practice. This Review synthesizes how bioacoustics
  contributes across the conservation workflow, from identifying threats and
  setting priorities to implementing interventions and evaluating their
  effectiveness, with particular emphasis on the Kunming–Montreal Global
  Biodiversity Framework. The authors examine the evidentiary strength of
  acoustic data, discuss advances in passive acoustic monitoring, machine
  learning and ecoacoustics, and provide guidance on selecting appropriate
  bioacoustic methods while accounting for uncertainty, reproducibility and
  ethical considerations. The Review aims to help conservation scientists
  and practitioners determine when bioacoustics is the appropriate approach,
  what types of conservation evidence it can provide, and how to apply these
  methods responsibly in biodiversity monitoring and decision-making.
